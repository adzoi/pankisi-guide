Pankisi Valley

A green gorge in Kakheti, Georgia, at the foot of the Greater Caucasus, home to the Kist people, old mosques, a Georgian church and some of the country's most unusual home cooking.

About the valley

Pankisi sits along the upper Alazani river, north of Akhmeta, in a string of villages: Duisi, Jokolo, Birkiani, Dzibakhevi, Omalo, Dumasturi, Khalatsani and Tsinubani. It is home to the Kists, Georgian citizens of Vainakh (Chechen) ancestry who settled here in the 19th century. Today visitors come for hiking, horse riding, community-run guesthouses and a culture where mosque, church and vineyard share the same landscape.

Food & drink

Kist cooking blends Chechen and Georgian traditions: flatbreads stuffed with pumpkin, handmade noodles with mutton, stone-ground maize bread, fresh cheeses and butter, wild herbs and nettles. Beyond that, the valley shares the wider Kakhetian table.

Visiting well

  • Book guesthouses and meals ahead; there are few restaurants.
  • Dress modestly, especially near mosques and when attending a zikr.
  • Ask before photographing people or religious gatherings.
  • Hiking and horse trips run best May to October; mountain huts are seasonal.
  • Use local guides for Batsara and the high plateau; permits are via the Agency of Protected Areas.
  • Akhmeta is the closest town for cash and supplies.
